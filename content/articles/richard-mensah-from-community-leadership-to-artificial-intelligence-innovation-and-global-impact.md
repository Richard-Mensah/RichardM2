The journey of a Ghanaian researcher, technology builder and institution leader seeking to connect knowledge, innovation and opportunity to the challenges facing communities across the world.

I have always believed that talent is everywhere, but opportunity is not. It is an idea that has shaped my decisions, influenced my leadership journey and continues to define the work I want to do.

My journey has taken me from community development initiatives in Ghana to academic research in artificial intelligence, international youth leadership, technology development and institution building. Although these experiences may appear to belong to different professional worlds, they are connected by one conviction: knowledge, technology and strong institutions should create opportunities that improve people's lives.

Where the journey began

My story is rooted in Ghana, particularly the communities and experiences that shaped my understanding of education, leadership and development.

Growing up, I became increasingly aware that people's opportunities were often determined not by their abilities or ambitions, but by the resources, institutions and support systems available to them.

I saw young people with enormous potential who lacked access to guidance, quality educational opportunities, professional networks and the confidence to pursue their ambitions.

These observations gradually shaped my interest in community development and youth empowerment.

In 2016, I became involved in community initiatives through Akosua Village in Winneba. My involvement in volunteer activities, educational campaigns and community engagement helped me understand that meaningful development begins with people and the institutions that support them.

I subsequently participated in youth leadership and civic engagement initiatives, including programmes associated with the Young African Leaders Initiative (YALI), peace advocacy and community education.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, when schools were disrupted, I contributed to organising free teaching sessions for children in communities around Sefwi Bekwai. These experiences reinforced my belief that education should not become inaccessible simply because circumstances are difficult.

For me, leadership was never simply about holding a position. It was about identifying a problem, taking responsibility and finding practical ways to contribute.

## Education, research and the pursuit of knowledge

My academic journey has been shaped by an interest in understanding people, institutions, technology and the systems that influence development. These academic experiences gave me different perspectives on human development, environmental systems, education and inclusion.

My interest in technology and analytical problem-solving eventually led me to pursue a Master of Science in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science at Bangor University in the United Kingdom, which I completed with Distinction.

This was an important development in my professional journey.

It provided an opportunity to move beyond observing development challenges and begin exploring how computational methods, data and artificial intelligence could help address them.

My master's research focused on predicting vehicle carbon dioxide emissions using machine learning. Working with Canada's Natural Resources Canada light-duty vehicle dataset, comprising 6,282 records, I explored predictive modelling approaches, including Random Forest, and used SHAP to examine the contribution of different vehicle features to model predictions.

The research strengthened my interest in explainable artificial intelligence, environmental analytics and evidence-based decision-making.

It also reinforced a question that continues to guide my intellectual interests:

How can we develop intelligent systems that are not only technically effective but also relevant, understandable and beneficial to the societies in which they operate?

Leadership beyond professional titles

My involvement in leadership and youth development has extended across community, national and international platforms.

Between 2020 and 2024, I served as a Volunteer Youth Member of Parliament with the United Nations Youth Association of Ghana. During this period, I represented the Western North Region and chaired the National Climate Policy Committee. These responsibilities exposed me to the relationship between young people's participation, public policy, environmental sustainability and institutional decision-making.

In 2024, I also contributed to climate-related development work through the GIZ and Center for Green Growth, working with the Korle-Klottey Municipal Assembly on climate solutions for Accra. I participated in initiatives that brought young people into conversations about climate change, sustainability and local development. Through related climate leadership activities, more than 300 young people were trained across Accra, Kumasi and Tamale.

These experiences helped me appreciate that sustainable development requires more than awareness campaigns. It requires research, institutional capacity, practical implementation, accountability and partnerships. They also strengthened my interest in the Sustainable Development Goals, particularly quality education, decent work, innovation, climate action, strong institutions and global partnerships.

Building institutions that outlive individuals

One of the most important dimensions of my journey has been institution building. I am the Founder and Global Director of EGA Mentorship International, an organisation established around a simple but important belief: young people should not have to navigate their ambitions without access to guidance, knowledge, networks and meaningful opportunities.

My involvement in youth development taught me that inspiration alone is insufficient. Young people need structured programmes, qualified mentors, practical skills, professional networks and pathways that connect learning to real opportunities.

EGA Mentorship International brings these elements together through mentorship, leadership development, skills training, international collaboration and opportunities aligned with sustainable development.

Returning to rebuild EGA in March 2026 marked an important chapter in my journey. It was an opportunity to rethink how an organisation could move beyond individual commitment and develop the structures required for long-term institutional growth.

My focus has increasingly shifted towards governance, organisational accountability, programme development, international partnerships and systems that preserve institutional knowledge.

I want to build an institution whose success is not dependent on my continued presence. Its values, structures, leadership and impact should be capable of surviving beyond its founder. That, to me, is the difference between establishing an organisation and building an institution.

Technology, innovation and the future I want to build

Alongside my academic and leadership responsibilities, I have developed a growing interest in software engineering, full-stack development and applied artificial intelligence.

I believe the next generation of development solutions will increasingly depend on the ability to combine research, software, data infrastructure and human understanding. My work in technology involves exploring and building digital platforms, web applications, data-driven solutions and systems that help individuals and organisations operate more effectively.

Through practical development projects, I have expanded my experience with technologies including Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, React, Next.js, PostgreSQL and modern application architectures.

I am particularly interested in the emerging relationship between large language models, intelligent agents, data infrastructure and the Model Context Protocol (MCP).

I see opportunities to develop systems that allow organisations to connect their information, tools and workflows more intelligently.

My broader ambition is to contribute to applied AI research and engineering, particularly in areas where technology can support education, institutional development, environmental intelligence and economic opportunity.

I do not see technology as an isolated professional interest. I see it as an instrument for solving problems that matter.

A commitment to the Global South

Much of my thinking is influenced by the development challenges and opportunities facing Africa and other regions of the Global South. I believe these regions should not be defined only by their development challenges or their position as consumers of technology developed elsewhere.

They should increasingly become contributors to research, innovation, technological advancement and global institutional decision-making.

Artificial intelligence, data science and digital transformation present important opportunities, but the benefits will not be distributed equally without investment in education, research capacity, infrastructure, governance and local innovation.

This is why I remain interested in the relationship between AI and development institutions.

I want to contribute to conversations about how emerging technologies can be developed responsibly, how institutions can become more effective and how communities can participate meaningfully in the digital economy.

My interests in climate intelligence, responsible AI, youth development and institutional design all connect to this broader ambition.

Looking ahead

My professional journey is still developing.

I am interested in advancing my research, contributing to meaningful technology projects, publishing academic work, collaborating with universities and research institutions, and exploring opportunities for doctoral research.

I also want to continue developing as a software engineer and AI practitioner while expanding my contribution to international development and institutional leadership.

I recognise that these ambitions require focus, discipline and continuous learning. I am increasingly interested in building a professional identity grounded in technical competence, credible research and practical execution.

I do not measure progress only by the positions I hold or the organisations I establish. I measure it by the problems I can help solve, the systems I can build, the knowledge I can contribute and the opportunities I can help create for others.

The journey from community volunteering to artificial intelligence research has taught me that meaningful impact does not come from choosing between technology and people. It comes from understanding how both can work together.

I am Richard Mensah. I am a researcher, technology builder, youth development advocate and institution builder.

My work continues to evolve, but the conviction behind it remains the same: talent exists everywhere, and with the right knowledge, institutions and opportunities, more people can participate in shaping the future.

The future I want to contribute to is one in which innovation expands opportunity, institutions serve people, and development is something communities actively create rather than simply receive.
